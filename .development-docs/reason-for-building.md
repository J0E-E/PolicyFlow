# Why I Built InsuraFlow

## Background

I recently went through an interview process for a Senior Full-Stack Engineer role at an insurance brokerage that is heavily investing in technology to modernize how customers purchase and manage insurance. The role involved building React/Python applications, integrating with Salesforce and other third-party systems, designing multi-tenant systems, handling sensitive customer data, and operating within a regulated environment.

Throughout the interview process, I felt that the role was a strong fit for my background. I have extensive experience with full-stack development, enterprise integrations, legacy modernization, and owning projects end-to-end. The conversations went well, and after the final stages they told me they believed I was capable of doing the work, but ultimately decided they were looking for someone with more directly transferable experience in CRM-centric systems and multi-tenant applications.

More specifically, the feedback centered around understanding the complexity of CRM object relationships and the integration patterns that surround those systems.

## Turning Feedback Into a Roadmap

Rather than viewing that feedback as a rejection, I treated it as a roadmap.

I followed up with the hiring manager to better understand what those gaps actually meant. Through those discussions, it became clear that the challenge wasn't learning Salesforce itself or implementing integrations, but understanding the business domain and the relationships between the underlying objects.

The complexity wasn't the APIs. The complexity was the business relationships and workflows built around them.

## Why InsuraFlow Exists

InsuraFlow is a multi-tenant insurance CRM simulation platform designed as both a deliberate learning exercise and a portfolio project.

The goal is to model realistic insurance brokerage workflows while demonstrating senior-level engineering concepts including multi-tenant architecture, secure handling of PII, auditability, event-driven architectures, background processing, CRM-adjacent integration patterns, and end-to-end ownership from design through deployment.

Instead of building a simple CRUD application or a Salesforce clone, the project focuses on the complexities of business relationships and the ecosystem of sidecar services that support them.

## The Goal

InsuraFlow serves two purposes:

1. Deepen my understanding of CRM domain modeling, insurance workflows, and enterprise integration patterns through hands-on experience.

2. Publicly demonstrate the ability to design, build, and operate the types of systems that modern, technology-driven organizations in regulated industries require.

What began as interview feedback became an opportunity to deliberately level up as an engineer by building expertise in an area that I found both professionally valuable and genuinely interesting.
